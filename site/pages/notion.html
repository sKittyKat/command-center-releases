Bringing in Notion

If your clients and projects live in Notion, bring them into Command Center once, check nothing was missed, then retire Notion. Command Center only ever reads Notion; it never writes there.

Connect

  1. At app.notion.com/developers/connections, as your workspace's owner, create an internal connection called Command Center.
  2. On its Configuration tab, let it read content only: untick updating and inserting content and any comment boxes, and choose no user information.
  3. On its Content access tab, pick every page and database you still use. A page's children come with it.
  4. Copy its API token (it starts ntn_), paste it into Settings › Sources › Notion import, and choose Connect Notion. The token goes to your keychain.

Import

The Notion import card lists each database it can see and what it'll become: Clients, Projects, Milestones, Contacts, or Not imported. Its guess is just a guess until you choose.

  • Each page shows whether it'll be added, matched to something you already have (and left as you typed it), or doesn't fit, with why.
  • Choose Import. It's one change, with one Undo in the action log.
  • Set aside any page with Not needed.

Keeping an eye on what's left

Until you retire it, Notion is read again when you choose Check Notion now and at each touchpoint, before the brief. A page stays live while it's waiting, doesn't fit, sits outside your databases unsettled, or changed in Notion since you settled it. Open any page in your browser from its row.

Retire Notion

The checklist has four lines. Two tick themselves: Notion read, with what you shared and Nothing live remains in Notion. Two you tick: Every page you still use is shared with the connection, and Your databases are locked in Notion (••• › Lock database). With all four ticked, choose Retire Notion. Command Center forgets the token, and the Sources row reads Retired.
